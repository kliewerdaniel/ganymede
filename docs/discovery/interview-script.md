# Ganymede — Interview Script

**Phase:** Discovery (Week 1)
**Purpose:** Map one real litigation workflow before showing anything.
**Rule:** Do not demo first. Ask them to walk through a recent matter task.

---

## Opening (every interview)

"Thank you for talking with me. I'm researching how civil-litigation teams handle document-heavy matters — specifically chronologies, internal memos, and fact-finding across PDFs, scans, and Word files. I'm not selling anything today. I'd like to understand one recent matter you worked on: what the documents were, what the job was, where the time went, and what review points existed. If I ask something that doesn't fit your practice, tell me — that's more useful than a polite answer."

---

## The matter walk-through (core)

These questions go deep on one recent matter. Pick the one they mention first that involved chronology, memo, or fact-finding work.

1. **Show me the last chronology or internal memo you built.** What was the matter, and what was the document set?
2. **Where did you spend the most time?** Be specific — searching filenames, reading to find a date, reconciling two versions, verifying a claim before drafting.
3. **Which documents were involved?** PDFs, scanned PDFs, Word files, emails (if any), notes. How many pages, roughly?
4. **How were the files organized?** Foldernames, naming conventions, who named them, inconsistencies.
5. **Who touched this matter?** Attorneys, paralegals, associates, support staff — and what each did.
6. **What was the review chain?** Who reviewed the chronology or memo before it was used or sent. What did they check for.
7. **What would make you distrust an answer immediately?** A wrong date, a source you can't find, a claim that isn't in the record.
8. **Which data can never leave your control?** Absolute boundary, not preference.
9. **Who approves a new tool and what evidence do they require?** Managing partner, IT, risk/counsel — and what they ask for.
10. **If you could only solve one expensive job with a tool like this, what would it be?** Don't let them say "everything."

---

## Current AI behavior (if it comes up)

11. **Have you used any AI tool on client or matter documents?** Which one. Under what conditions.
12. **What did you check before using it?** If they didn't check anything, that's the answer.
13. **What would have to be true for you to use an AI tool on a real matter file?** Specific conditions, not vague comfort.

---

## Buying path

14. **Who would need to sign off on a tool like this?** Name the roles, not just the person.
15. **What evidence would they want before approving?** Security review, data flow, citations, audit trail, references, pilot terms.
16. **Is there a budget owner or procurement path?** If no, that's a disqualifier.

---

## Closing

17. **What would make you walk away from a tool like this after one use?**
18. **Is there a matter you could share for a pilot that has no client-confidentiality barrier?** Synthetic, public, or expressly authorized — not live client data without written terms.
19. **Would you be willing to review a prototype or a citation sample before committing?** Yes / no / "show me the workflow first."

---

## What counts as useful evidence

**Weak:** "Interesting," newsletter signup, social reaction, feature suggestion.

**Useful:** Introduces IT, offers a corpus, schedules reviewers, shares current cost.

**Strong:** Reviews terms, names budget, signs pilot, or pays.

---

## Disqualifiers (check during or after interview)

- The only interest is "AI is exciting."
- No one will supply a safe corpus or review outputs.
- The desired use case requires licensed legal content on day one.
- The buyer expects autonomous legal advice or guaranteed accuracy.
- Every prospect demands a different first integration.

If you hit one of these, record it and move on. Do not try to overcome a disqualifier with persuasion.

---

## After the interview

Write up:
- The workflow: trigger, inputs, actors, work, review, output.
- The economic baseline: people, hours, frequency, delay, write-offs.
- The risk baseline: where confidential data currently travels.
- The buying map: sponsor, approver, IT/security reviewer, end user, budget owner.
- Whether a safe corpus is available.
- Whether there is a champion.

File in `docs/discovery/<interview-date>-<firm-identifier>.md`.
