## Post-pilot pricing hypothesis

| Offer | Price hypothesis | Includes |
|-------|-----------------|----------|
| Core annual | $24,000 / year | 10 users, software, updates, support |
| Additional user | $150 / month | Named user license |
| Implementation | $7,500 once | Install, policy setup, training, acceptance |
| Managed dedicated host | $12,000 / year | Isolated environment; infra billed separately |

Pilot credit toward an annual agreement and final packaging are sales tests. Prices exclude customer hardware, cloud infrastructure, unusual migration, and bespoke integrations.

---

## Expected initial contract

A 15-user firm on customer-provided infrastructure produces $33,000 in annual recurring revenue: $24,000 platform plus five additional users at $1,800 each. Implementation adds $7,500 in the first year. Managed dedicated hosting can lift recurring value where the customer wants isolation without operating the stack.

---

## Pricing principles

- Preserve a platform floor; support and security work do not scale with seat count alone.
- Use annual contracts after the pilot; monthly cancellation is inconsistent with deployment effort.
- Charge separately for non-standard integration, migration, and dedicated operational responsibility.
- Do not publish fixed tiers until at least ten buyer conversations reveal budget and packaging objections.

---

## Price as a test

The $4,500 pilot is an initial hypothesis designed to filter for a real buyer and pay for high-touch onboarding. Test the reaction in Week 1. If buyers reject it, identify whether the objection is price, trust, procurement, or insufficient value before discounting.
