## Sell an outcome with a clear boundary

A paid pilot qualifies demand, covers high-touch onboarding, and gives the firm a controlled way to test value and risk before an annual commitment.

---

## Expected initial contract

A 15-user firm on customer-provided infrastructure produces $33,000 in annual recurring revenue: $24,000 platform plus five additional users at $1,800 each. Implementation adds $7,500 in the first year. Managed dedicated hosting can lift recurring value where the customer wants isolation without operating the stack.

---

## Pricing principles

- Preserve a platform floor; support and security work do not scale with seat count alone.
- Use annual contracts after the pilot; monthly cancellation is inconsistent with deployment effort.
- Charge separately for non-standard integration, migration, and dedicated operational responsibility.
- Do not publish fixed tiers until at least ten buyer conversations reveal budget and packaging objections.

## Pilot success scorecard

- **3+** active users in two weeks.
- **20+** meaningful tasks.
- **70%** of outputs useful after edits.
- Median reported time saved of at least 30% on the chosen workflow.
- At least 90% of material claims supported by reviewed sources.
- No cross-matter access failure and no unresolved critical security defect.
- A written continuation decision or a specific, categorized loss reason.

## Customer success

Implementation is part of the product until it is automated.

### Standard implementation

| Day | Work | Acceptance |
|-----|------|------------|
| 0-2 | Security, corpus, roles, environment | Data path approved |
| 3-5 | Install, model, backup, administrator | Health and restore pass |
| 6-8 | Ingest pilot matter; reconcile failures | Corpus manifest signed off |
| 9 | User training on three workflows | Guided tasks completed |
| 10-30 | Use, office hours, quality review | Scorecard complete |

**Support promise:** Business-hours support for the core plan, with a published severity model and response target. Diagnostic collection excludes matter content and secrets by default. Critical confidentiality, authorization, or data-loss events pause use and follow the contractual incident path.

**Adoption design:** Train users on verification and limits, not prompt tricks. Seed three guided tasks based on the firm's own workflow. Show citations before prose and keep the source inspector one click away. Collect "supporting / weak / wrong / inaccessible" feedback on citations. Review low-rated outputs with the user who performed the work.

**Renewal and expansion:** Begin renewal review 90 days before term end. Expand only when active users, repeated workflows, and sponsor value are visible. Additional practice groups should receive their own corpus, roles, benchmark, and success criteria.

## Unit economics

Base case reaches operating profitability in Year 3.

| Base-case metric | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|-----------------|--------|--------|--------|
| Average recurring contract | $33k | $36k | $40k |
| Gross margin | 72% | 78% | 82% |
| Acquisition cost assumption | $8k | $12k | $14k |
| CAC payback | 4.0 mo | 5.1 mo | 5.1 mo |
| Annual gross retention target | 90% | 92% | 93% |

All figures are internal planning assumptions. CAC includes allocated founder sales time, travel, events, and marketing. Payback uses recurring gross profit only.

### Margin improvement plan

- **Standard deployment:** one supported reference profile, automated preflight, signed release, migration test, and rollback.
- **Content-safe support:** redacted diagnostics and health checks reduce the need to inspect matter data.
- **Benchmark automation:** every update runs the same parsing, retrieval, citation, permission, latency, and recovery tests.
- **Paid exceptions:** unusual document migration, infrastructure, integrations, and service levels are scoped separately.

### Warning signals

Pause growth if:
- Implementation exceeds 40 founder hours per account.
- Support exceeds four hours per customer per month after onboarding.
- Gross margin stays below 65%.
- Every firm requires a different architecture.

**Economic moat:** the same trust promise must become cheaper to deliver with every customer. If the company can only sell control through founder heroics, it is a consultancy.

## Scenario analysis

| Year 3 scenario | Downside | Base | Upside |
|----------------|----------|------|--------|
| Year-end customers | 30 | 75 | 120 |
| Exit recurring contract | $32k | $40k | $45k |
| Exit ARR | $0.96M | $3.00M | $5.40M |
| Gross margin | 65% | 82% | 85% |
| Operating posture | Narrow / conserve | Profitable growth | Fund expansion |

Scenario values are management assumptions. They are shown to expose sensitivity, not as probability-weighted forecasts.

### Downside response

If deployments remain bespoke, sales cycles exceed 120 days, or users do not return, freeze hiring and narrow to the one artifact with repeat use. Maintain paid implementation and avoid subsidizing custom integrations.

### Base response

If pilot conversion, gross retention, and support load stay within plan, hire reliability and customer-success capacity, publish an approved case study, and expand through referrals and technical partners.

### Upside response

If inbound and partner demand exceed delivery capacity, consider institutional capital to accelerate assurance, deployment automation, and channel development. Do not widen the product before reliability keeps pace.

**Break-even guide:** At a $36,000 annual recurring contract and 78% gross margin, roughly 24 active accounts support a $670,000 annual operating-cost base before non-recurring implementation contribution.

Raise only enough to prove repeatability.

|  |  |

| Planning runway | 18 months |

### Use of funds

| Category | Allocation | Purpose |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| Founder runway + engineering | 43% | Product, benchmark, deployment |
| Specialists | 17% | Security, UX, legal-domain input |
| Security + legal + insurance | 16% | Contracts, review, test, coverage |
| Sales + customer work | 14% | Travel, events, pilots, content |
| Reserve | 10% | Hardware, delays, incident buffer |

### Financing options

- **Bootstrap:** preserve ownership and use pilot/implementation revenue; accept slower assurance and hiring.
- **Angel / pre-seed:** finance 12-18 months of proof with investors who understand legal technology and security.
- **Strategic design partners:** use prepaid annual contracts or paid co-development only when product rights and roadmap control remain clear.

### Next financing gate

Do not raise a larger round on prototype interest. Seek scale capital only after at least six annual customers, a documented renewal signal, a repeatable deployment, a measured gross-margin path, and evidence that the pipeline exceeds founder capacity.

## Decision framework

|  | Deepen | Narrow |
|--|--------|--------|
| Trigger | Repeated use, quality pass, paid continuation. | One artifact wins. Remove the rest and make that job the product. |
| Next move | Improve the same workflow and onboard two referrals. | Improve the same workflow and onboard two referrals. |

|  | Fix | Stop |
|--|-----|------|
| Trigger | Demand exists, but quality or deployment misses. | No recurring use or budget after valid trials. |
| Next move | Run a reliability sprint with no new features. | Preserve assets and choose a new vertical. |

**Definition of success:** not a finished app, but a product real firms pay to use, a benchmark that exposes its limits, a deployment another operator can run, and a clear reason to build the next version.
